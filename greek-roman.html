Ouranian and chthonic ▾

Ouranian

The Ouranian gods are the powers of the bright sky and the Olympian court. They were honored by day, with white or light-colored animals sacrificed at raised altars. The gods' portion was burned, the worshippers shared the meat, and prayers were offered with hands raised toward the sky.

Chthonic

The chthonic gods (pronounced THON-ik, from the Greek word for "earth") are the powers of the ground and the underworld. They were honored at dusk or night, with dark animals offered at low altars or in pits. The offering was often burned whole or its blood poured out, with no shared meal, and libations went down into the earth. Chthonic doesn't mean evil. These gods govern the dead, but also grain, fertility, and the wealth that grows from the ground.

A note on the split

The split is a way scholars describe differences in worship, and the Greeks didn't draw it as sharply as the labels suggest. Many gods belonged to both: Zeus was worshipped as Zeus Chthonios, and Hermes guided souls to the underworld. The "Sphere" label on each entry is a lean, not a rule.

A note on festival dates ▾

Roman festivals fell on fixed calendar dates, so those are exact. Greek festivals followed city calendars based on the moon, and the Athenian months drift against ours, so their timing is approximate. Months are given by name with a modern range, and days are given where they're known. "Monthly" lines are days of every month that were sacred to a god.
