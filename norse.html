Where the stories come from ▾

Almost everything we know about the Norse gods comes from the Poetic Edda and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda, both written down in Iceland in the 1200s by Christians, centuries after the old religion faded. The poems are older than the manuscripts, but the versions we have were shaped by the people who wrote them down. Treat any single detail as one voice among several.

Aesir and Vanir ▾

The Norse gods come in two families. The Aesir are the gods of rule, law, war and sky, led by Odin. The Vanir are tied to fertility, wealth, the sea and the earth's abundance. In the myths the two groups fought a war and ended it with a truce, an exchange of hostages and a shared spit that became Kvasir. After that they live as one community, so the line between them is looser than the Greek split between Olympian and chthonic. The giants (jotnar) are a third group, older and often hostile, but gods marry into them, and some of the most important figures, such as Loki and Skadi, belong to both worlds.

A note on festivals ▾

Only three major sacrifices are firmly attested, from Snorri: Winter Nights in October, Yule at midwinter, and the start of summer (Sigrblot) around April. Most of the other festival dates you will see online are modern Heathen reconstructions, and I have marked them as modern where they appear. Where no festival is attested, the entry says so.
